# Game film — norming before anyone scores anyone

Peer and self scoring only work after rater training (peer–teacher agreement
rises sharply with it). This protocol is that training. Run it the first time
any skill/rubric row enters the scoring rotation, and again whenever the
monthly audit finds drift.

## Materials

Three College Board sample responses for the target task from AP Central's
scoring materials — one high, one middle, one low — plus the official scoring
commentary. Anonymize and strip the awarded scores before class. (CB samples
are copyrighted: link/handout in class, don't republish.)

## The norming session (~25 minutes, fits a Type C block's worked-example slot)

1. **Read the rubric row(s) aloud** — just the rows in play today. Students
   restate each in their own words with a partner (2 min).
2. **Score silently** (8 min): each student scores all three papers on the
   target rows only, writing one sentence of rubric language per decision.
3. **Commit publicly** (2 min): hands/cards for each paper's score — the
   spread IS the lesson.
4. **Argue to the text** (8 min): where scores diverge, students must point at
   rubric words and paper words. David referees with the scoring commentary,
   revealing the real scores row by row.
5. **Calibration close** (3 min): each student writes "the decision I got
   wrong and the rubric line that settles it" in their tracker.

## After norming: the peer-scoring contract

- Scorer signs their score. Scores are formative, never in Schoology.
- Rubric-row-limited: one or two rows per session, announced in advance.
- Disagreement protocol: scorer and writer argue to the rubric text for two
  minutes; unresolved → the paper goes in David's spot-score pile.

## Self-scoring by sample-matching (every 2–3 weeks)

Hand back three anonymized class responses to the same prompt. Students rank
them, justify the ranking in rubric language, then locate their own response
in the ranking. The placement error — not the score — is the feedback.

## Monthly calibration audit

David blind re-scores 3–4 peer-scored papers (use `grading-stress-test` for
his own second opinion). Discrepancy > 1 point on any row → that row gets
re-normed at the next Type C block. Log audit results in the course close-out
notes so drift is visible across the year.
