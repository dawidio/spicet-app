# The flipped pipeline — content acquisition with accountability

The rule that makes flipping work (the consistent moderator in the
meta-analyses): **a pre-class check exists for every assignment, and the freed
class time goes to active practice.** Unaccountable reading gets ~20–30%
compliance; checks roughly double it.

## Assignment format (every content block's pre-work)

1. **Source**: Heimler topic video (verify campus streaming) or WHP AP article
   for the CED topic — David's `whp-ap-alignment` reference maps these. AP
   Daily is the backup. One topic per block, 10–20 minutes of material, never
   more.
2. **Prequestions on the exit slide the block before**: 2–3 questions handed
   out BEFORE the video — prequestioning improves retention of what follows.
   Wrong guesses are fine and expected.
3. **Accountability artifact** (pick one per assignment):
   - **Entry check** (default): 3–5 items, first 5 minutes of class or due
     digitally before class. AP Classroom Topic Questions when a matching
     topic exists; otherwise an autograded Schoology quiz.
   - **Retrieval guide**: students complete from memory after watching, then
     check against the video — spot-checked at the door.
   - **One-sentence claim**: submit a single because/but/so sentence about the
     topic to Schoology before class.

## Grading policy

- Entry checks: **completion credit only** in Schoology. Grading accuracy
  teaches students to copy; completion + visible class-level results keeps the
  data honest and the stakes low (low stakes is what the retrieval evidence
  wants).
- The class-level item analysis drives minutes 17–30 of the Studio skeleton:
  clarify what was missed, skip what wasn't.
- No pre-work completed → the student does the entry check cold and the
  result stands; the natural consequence is the accountability. No separate
  punishment economy.

## What is banned as homework

Unaccountable textbook reading, Cornell-notes-for-points, definition copying,
and "study for the test." Homework is only ever: pre-work + check, scheduled
relearning (app Review mode), one component write or 90-second recorded
defense, prequestion sheets.
