## Part 2 — The Spiral Studio, compressed

**Five commitments:** (1) content acquisition moves home (video/article + a
mandatory accountability check — flipping is arithmetic on 70 days);
(2) every block opens with 10–12 min cumulative retrieval, half from ≥2 units
back, immediate feedback, knew/lucky/wrong calibration marks; (3) writing
trained in components daily (because/but/so sentence sprints) and weekly
(SAQ/thesis/paragraph reps), full essays ~1/unit, scored at scale via
norming → peer → self → teacher spot-score; (4) everything spirals — IDs/dates
on a successive-relearning schedule (3-correct mastery, then 7d → 14d → 28d
passes; the app's Review mode runs this), unit checks 30% lagged, review
season = the spiral's final lap; (5) every major written product defended
orally 5–8 min, reasoning-scored (also the AI-integrity architecture).

**Five day types:** A Studio (default: opener → sentence sprint →
clarification-not-lecture → studio block → writing rep → exit+prequestions) ·
B Document Day (one central question, HAPP on pre-excerpted sources, ACE
close) · C Writing Workshop (worked example → timed write → score-and-argue) ·
D Summit/Defense (3 stations, trained peer panelists, 8-min slots) ·
E Spiral Day (full-period mixed retrieval + repair; default sub plan).

**Calendar allocation (APWHM):** U1–2: 9 blocks · U3–6: 32 (the exam's 48–60%
band) · U7–9: 15 · review: 12; content done early April. APUSH: Periods 3–8
are the heavy band (~74%).

**Key evidence anchors** (for justifying recommendations): retrieval practice
classroom g ≈ 0.50, ≈ a letter grade in real history classrooms
(Roediger/Agarwal 2011); feedback nearly doubles it (Rowland 2014); optimal
review gap ≈ 10–20% of retention interval → 2–4 week cycles for May (Cepeda
2008); self-regulated flashcards lose the effect — the imposed schedule IS the
intervention (Rawson & Dunlosky); interleaving works only for confusable
categories (Brunmair & Richter 2019); 2025 Chief Reader DBQ point-earn rates:
thesis .80, doc evidence 1.61/2, **context .54, sourcing .29, outside evidence
.19, complexity .17** — the missing points are context/sourcing/outside
evidence, and complexity is near-irrelevant to a 5; a 5 ≈ 73% composite —
banked cheap points at volume, not perfection; ~48–50% of takers now earn 4–5
post-recalibration, so 4s/5s are the target.

## Part 3 — THE COMPARISON PROTOCOL (your task)

### Step 0 — Inventory
List what David attached (lessons, decks, unit plans, assessments, pacing,
homework). Note coverage: which units, which artifact types. If he attached
one unit, say the comparison generalizes only as far as that unit is
representative — and ask him if it is.

### Step 1 — Audit against the ten dimensions
Score each dimension **Already Spiral** (present in his materials) /
**Convertible** (present but needs re-housing) / **Missing**. Quote his actual
materials as evidence for every rating — no rating without a citation from his
own work.

1. **Retrieval presence** — do lessons open with recall from memory (not
   review-by-reread)? Is any of it *lagged* (≥2 units back)?
2. **Spacing** — does content return on any schedule after its unit ends?
   Cumulative unit tests? Anything resembling relearning passes?
3. **First exposure location** — is new content delivered by in-class
   lecture/deck, or moved to pre-work?
4. **Accountability** — does assigned pre-work/reading have a check? What
   compliance mechanism exists?
5. **Writing rep volume & grain** — how many writing touches per week? Full
   essays vs components (thesis-only, context-only, single paragraphs)?
6. **Scoring modes** — who scores what? Any peer/self scoring? Any norming
   against CB samples before students score?
7. **Exam-weighted time** — does the pacing spend blocks proportional to CED
   unit weights? Review season length and structure?
8. **2027-format readiness** — non-text source (SAQ3) practice? No
   LEQ-choice-dependent strategies? Whole-course DBQ evidence range?
9. **Point-targeting** — do writing materials drill the scarce points
   (contextualization, sourcing-to-argument, outside evidence) or
   overweight thesis/complexity?
10. **Integrity/defense architecture** — process artifacts, classroom-anchored
    evidence, oral defense (his AI-resistant redesigner pattern) — how
    systematically applied?

### Step 2 — The two-way verdict
Write it honestly in both directions:
- **Where his materials beat the generic design** — content knowledge, source
  choices, local resonance (Shanghai/China connections), activities that
  demonstrably work with his students. These get PRESERVED, and you should
  flag them for backporting into the playbook (`docs/` in the spicet-app
  repo). The Spiral Studio is a frame; his materials are the pictures.
- **Where the design beats his materials** — cite the dimension, the evidence
  anchor, and the size of the expected difference. No vague "consider
  adding"; say what changes and what it buys.
- **The one-sentence answer** to his actual question: is the system better
  than what he built, for which dimensions, by how much, at what conversion
  cost.

### Step 3 — Convert one lesson (proof, not promise)
Pick the most representative attached lesson and re-house it, before/after:
warm-up → lagged opener (write the actual items, half from earlier units) ·
lecture segment → pre-work assignment + 3–5 item entry check (write it) · his
source/discussion work → the Main Event, kept nearly verbatim · append a
component writing rep with its scoring mode · exit ticket → exit + prequestions
for the next topic. Output as .docx, Calibri, David's voice (his `david-voice`
skill governs), showing OLD and NEW side by side with a "what changed and why"
margin. Target: the conversion demonstrates ~80% of his material survives
untouched.

### Step 4 — Effort estimate + adoption path
From what you actually saw (not the generic estimate): per-lesson conversion
minutes, one-time setup items (ID deck curation from CED illustrative
examples, CB anchor sets for norming, Schoology categories, tracker), and a
phased path — daily skeleton first (biggest single effect), flip by Unit 1–2,
norming/peer scoring by Unit 3, full calendar by the heavy band. Offer the
two-course A/B option (full system in one course, partial in the other,
compare in July).
